# Does parallax suffice to recover unseen depth without depth labels?

Make four worlds with the identical first image. Four separate textured planar objects occupy four distinct depth levels. Cycle the depths across the quartet; each foreground pixel has all four possible depths with the same reference appearance. A fixed single-view depth guess therefore scores exactly 25% across the quartet. Render a translated second view by calibrated horizontal perspective disparity and z-buffering. Train a shared two-layer patch matcher from scratch only on the expected absolute difference between corresponding image intensities. The matcher receives pairs of raw local patches at the four candidate projections. It receives no depth maps, object IDs, mesh labels, occlusion masks, or per-pixel target disparities during optimization. Projection geometry and the four allowed inverse depths are supplied.

Keep one learned arm, its matched untrained initialization, and a cheap deterministic stereo control: pick the candidate with the smallest mean absolute intensity difference over the same patch. Compare a fixed monocular guess, and verify that forward-projecting the renderer's true depths exactly reproduces the new view. Use three paired model seeds, 400 Adam updates, eight fresh training scenes per update, 32 sampled foreground pixels per scene, a 32-unit hidden layer, and no hyperparameter selection. A foreground pixel is any nonblack reference pixel. Train on rectangles and ellipses with signed unit camera motion. Test a fresh in-distribution panel and a shift panel with unseen triangle/diamond shapes and twice the camera translation. Each panel has 96 independent layouts, with four counterfactual depths per layout. The primary measure is exact four-level depth accuracy on all reference foreground pixels in the shift panel. Also score visible and occluded pixels, flat and textured regions, and reference-only versus inferred-depth rendering of a new third view on the opposite side of the scene at triple unit displacement. Use a paired bootstrap over layouts and seeds. Controls use identical held-out images.

Prediction: the trained matcher improves on its initial weights and beats the single-view bound by at least ten percentage points on the shifted panel. If the fixed patch matcher matches or beats the learner, prefer the analytic method for this renderer; do not claim a learned advantage. The second view contains depth cues by construction. The experiment addresses the need for explicit depth labels on this task, not the emergence of 3D understanding in unconstrained models.
