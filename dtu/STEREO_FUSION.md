# Can two real photos recover depths that both individual matches miss?

The earlier DTU test found 1.27 and 0.37 percentage points of headroom for picking the better single extra camera on each reference after seeing the answers. Both cameras still might carry complementary evidence about the same depth. The question here is whether combining their evidence before choosing a depth recovers more than selecting or averaging after each camera has already committed to one answer. Multiview stereo has established photometric and geometric evidence aggregation as a method family. This test holds the two photographed views and projection model fixed. [Earlier DTU pilot](PILOT_RESULTS.md) · [DTU dataset](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/html/Jensen_Large_Scale_Multi-view_2014_CVPR_paper.html) · [Schönberger et al., pixelwise view selection](https://demuc.de/papers/schoenberger2016mvs.pdf)

Use the earlier official scans 1 and 6, twelve archived common-visible camera triplets per scan, and the exact same 512 reference pixels per triplet. Confirm that the published 3×3 patch predictions reproduce on both side cameras. Reuse the calibrated plane sweep and 96 inverse-depth candidates, with 7×7 patches for every new arm. The same larger patch gives the single-view controls better context. For each side camera compute zero-mean normalized cross-correlation at every valid depth. Compare the horizontal and vertical visibility-matched cameras individually, the arithmetic mean of their selected depths, and the depth maximizing the equally weighted mean of both full score curves. The joint method requires a valid patch in both views at that depth. Average-of-estimates and joint costs both consume the same three photos. They differ in when they discard alternative depth hypotheses. Structured-light measurements select the benchmark's common-visible points and supply the scoring depth. The matching step receives only the projected pixels, calibration and photographs.

Measure accuracy within 10 mm in the two scenes separately and bootstrap complete reference-camera triplets within each scene. Advance only if the joint method improves by at least four percentage points over the strongest of the two fixed single-camera arms and the same-budget mean-of-estimates arm in both scenes, with positive lower paired bounds. Record pixels recovered when both single-camera estimates fail, pixels lost where either single camera is right, and median absolute errors. This is a diagnostic of joint depth estimation on two tabletop scans with common visibility. It does not test the generality of a learned 3D representation or occluded surfaces.
